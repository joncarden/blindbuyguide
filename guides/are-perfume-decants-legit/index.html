Are perfume decants legit?

Short answer: Yes, decants from established sellers are a widely used way to try expensive fragrance before committing; they're portions of genuine bottles transferred into small vials, so the risk is the seller, not the format.

Yes, from a good seller. A decant is the real fragrance, poured from a full bottle into a small atomizer. It's how most fragrance nerds try things before committing, and it's why a 1 ml of Creed Aventus can cost $6.99 when the bottle is $495.

The format is fine. The question is always the seller.

How to pick a decant seller

  • Track record and volume. Established shops with years of reviews and a big catalogue have the most to lose from selling fakes.
  • Clear labels. Fragrance name, house, concentration and size on every vial.
  • Prices in line with everyone else. Our library shows a median of $6.99 for 1 ml and $12.99 for 2 ml across niche and luxury scents. A seller far below that on a $400 fragrance deserves suspicion.
  • Returns and customer service you can actually reach.
  • Fresh stock. Decants of discontinued or hard-to-find scents are where fakes and old stock are most common.

A note on the legal side

Brands don't authorize decanting, and lawyers describe reselling repackaged fragrance as a legal grey area for the seller. Buying a decant for your own use isn't the issue; we just think you should know why you won't find decants at the brand's own store.

Verdict: decants from reputable sellers? Run, don't walk. They're the single best tool for not wasting $300.
