What it smells like, per the note list

The notes as listed: top: Litchi, Rhubarb, Bergamot, Nutmeg; heart: Turkish Rose, Peony, Musk, Petalia, Vanilla; base: Cashmeran, Incense, Cedar, Haitian Vetiver. Retailers disagree slightly: one page lists incense in the base, another lists frankincense and omits peony and petalia, so check the brand page before you buy.

Wearers commonly describe a polished, fruity rose: lychee and rhubarb up top, rose and peony in the middle, with a soft vetiver and cedar base. It is very popular, which cuts both ways. The note list reads sweet-pink more than dark-rose.

Who it's worth it for, and who should pass

Worth it if: you like fruity-pink florals and want a bottle that reads polished. Think Bridgerton ballroom, not Bridgerton scandal.

Pass if: you dislike sweet fruit with your rose, or you want a scent that is rare on your feed.
