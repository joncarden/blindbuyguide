Rarely. The median 2 ml sample in our library costs **$12.99** (Nov 14, 2026). The median in-stock full bottle costs about **$3.90 per ml**, which puts a typical 100 ml niche bottle at around $390. One option risks a sandwich. The other risks a month's car insurance.

## Our rule: the $150 line

Blind buy only if **all** of these are true:

1. The bottle is under about $150.
2. You already love a scent that wearers consistently compare it to.
3. You've read the notes and none of them are your known enemies (for many people: oud, heavy patchouli, powdery iris, or anything described as "animalic").
4. The retailer takes returns, or you'd be fine gifting it.

Above $150, sample first. Every time. Even if TikTok is screaming.

Want the bottles that pass? Here is [the short, honest list of safe blind buys](/guides/safe-blind-buy-perfumes/).

## Why note lists lie (a little)

A note list tells you the ingredients that the brand chose to advertise, not how they smell together on your skin after four hours. "Pineapple" in Creed Aventus and "pineapple" in a body spray are not the same experience. And the drydown, the part that stays on your skin all afternoon, is the part a paper strip in a store never shows you.

## How to stop blind buying

- **Order three to five 1 ml samples at once.** Shipping is usually the same for one or five.
- **Do side-by-sides.** One scent per wrist. Check at 10 minutes, 2 hours, 6 hours.
- **Wear the winner for a full day,** then a second day. If you're still thinking about it on day three, buy the bottle (from [the shop that actually has it](/guides/same-perfume-different-price/), obviously).

Popular examples: a 1 ml of [Creed Aventus](/scents/creed-aventus/) is $6.99 at Fragrances Line, where the 100 ml bottle is listed at $495; a 2 ml of [Baccarat Rouge 540](/scents/baccarat-rouge-540-edp/) is $18.99 against a $270 listing for 70 ml. (Both bottles were sold out there when we checked; the samples were not.)

**Verdict:** blind buying a $400 niche fragrance off a note list? Absolutely not. Sample it first.
